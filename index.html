Research agenda

From user needs to adaptive interaction

My work has progressively moved from understanding what people need, to identifying uncertainty in what they express, and finally to designing agents that update individual user models over time.

This trajectory connects design research, empirical HCI, and computational prototyping around one question: how can an intelligent system adapt without making the person lose understanding or control?

  1. 01 · 2024

    Understand situated needs

    Interviews, questionnaires, personas, and journey mapping revealed how context shapes user needs.

  2. 02 · 2024–25

    Identify evolving demands

    Computational analysis translated large-scale, fragmented feedback into traceable product-design priorities.

  3. 03 · 2025–26

    Respond to uncertain intent

    A controlled experiment examined how ambiguity and response explainability jointly shape user experience.

  4. 04 · Current

    Adapt through interaction

    A personal-agent prototype records feedback, updates individual preference profiles, and adapts later decisions.

About

Design background, empirical methods, and computational systems.

I am an M.Eng. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, with a bachelor's background in Architecture. This path shaped my interest in how people understand, negotiate, and retain control over increasingly intelligent environments.

My work combines human-centered inquiry with computational implementation: interviews and experiments help define the problem, while Python, multi-agent systems, and immersive simulation help me examine possible solutions.
